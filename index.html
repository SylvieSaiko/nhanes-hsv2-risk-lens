Before interpreting a result

Know what this estimate cannot do.

It does not diagnose HSV-2.

Only appropriate laboratory testing and clinical evaluation can inform diagnosis. A modeled probability does not establish active lesions, shedding, symptoms, or transmission risk.

It is not a recommendation for routine screening.

U.S. guidance recommends against routine serologic screening of asymptomatic adolescents and adults. People with symptoms, a partner with genital herpes, HIV, or other relevant clinical circumstances should discuss testing with a clinician.

It applies only to the study population.

The models were developed among U.S. adults aged 20-49 with no reported prior clinician-diagnosed genital herpes. Public-use adult HSV-2 data ended after 2015-2016, so contemporary transportability remains unknown.

Absolute probabilities may be too high in later populations.

Temporal-validation calibration intercepts were negative, consistent with declining HSV-2 prevalence. The website shows the original development-fit probability and does not reuse validation data for recalibration.
